# Vendor and EV fidelity

Can the simulator stand in for **a specific charger make and model**, and drive it with **a specific
EV**, closely enough that a CSMS cannot tell the difference in the tests that matter? This page answers
that from the code on `main` at `86ac0f2e` (2026-11-09), dimension by dimension, and lists what is
missing in the order that unlocks the most real test needs. It builds on the
[charger engine](../concepts/charger-engine.md) (behaviors and params), the
[EV and cable model](../concepts/charging-sessions.md#ev-and-cable-model),
[vehicle profiles](../concepts/vehicle-profiles.md) and the earlier
[OECS analysis](oecs-as-a-charger-model-source.md), which covers the charger-datasheet import in depth and
is not repeated here. Protocol conformance (which OCPP test cases pass) is a separate question; an
analysis of it is in progress on the branch `docs/ocpp-coverage-analysis` (`docs/analyses/ocpp-coverage.md`,
not on `main` yet).

Some work that changes the EV side is merged into the `integ/next` integration branch but **not** into
`main` at the time of writing: vehicle behaviors (#835: `packages/vehicle`, `EVSettings.vehicleBehavior`) and
station power sharing with a vendor current-limit key and DC conversion efficiency (#608). Rows it affects say
"in flight (`integ/next`)".

## Verdict

- **(a) A specific charger model: partially, and only by hand.** Everything a CSMS observes _can_ be shaped —
  boot identity, connector count, per-connector power cap, meter realism, which actions are implemented,
  firmware / log / reset / unlock timings and failures, any inbound answer, any outbound payload — but through
  five different surfaces (`cp.create` fields, behavior params, TypeScript behaviors, scenario nodes,
  configuration writes at run time). The main blockers: **configuration keys and 2.x variables cannot be seeded
  at creation** (a station always answers `GetConfiguration` with the simulator's defaults and rejects keys it
  does not know), **several advertised limits are not enforced** (`MinimumStatusDuration` is never read), and
  **there is no packaged "model profile"** that bundles all five surfaces into one reusable, file-loadable unit.
- **(b) A specific EV: battery, power and DC curve yes; electrical and protocol behavior largely no.** A vehicle
  profile imported from Open EV Directory gives capacity, AC/DC maximum power, AC phase count and a published DC
  kW-vs-SoC curve, and the session honours the charging profile, the charger cap and the curve together. Missing:
  AC current limits per phase and onboard-charger behavior, a DC voltage/current model (voltage is one constant),
  temperature, the EV's own ramp and reaction delays, ISO 15118 / Plug & Charge identity and charging needs, and
  V2G. On `main` the EV does not decide anything by itself (pause, delayed start, target reached → `SuspendedEV`);
  #835 adds that and is in flight.

"Comprehensively" is therefore not possible today for either side. For the common CSMS test needs — "behave like
model X at boot, in `GetConfiguration`, under smart charging and in firmware updates, with car Y's power curve"
— the gap is mostly packaging and seeding, not missing simulation.

## Method and scope

**A specific charger model** here means its _observable OCPP behavior_ — what it sends, when, with which
payloads, how it answers each CSMS call (including where it deviates from the specification), which
configuration keys / device-model variables it exposes with which defaults and mutability — plus its
_electrical behavior_ as seen in meter values (power per connector, phases, measurands, ramp, power sharing).
**A specific EV** means its electrical acceptance (capacity, SoC, power vs SoC, AC phases and currents, DC
voltage and current), its session behavior (when it asks for energy, pauses, stops, unplugs) and its protocol
identity where the station relays it (ISO 15118 contract certificates, charging needs, V2G).

A simulator can reproduce the **wire**: every byte, timing and state transition a CSMS can observe. It cannot
reproduce what does not reach the wire (enclosure, display, RFID reader physics, contactor wear, real metering
accuracy, the EV–EVSE link below OCPP), and it can only approximate physics that has no published data (battery
thermal behavior). Each row below is judged against the wire.

Legend: **Yes** — configurable without code; **Behavior** — possible, needs a TypeScript
[behavior](../concepts/charger-engine.md#behavior-api) (code, loaded with `--behavior`); **Scenario** — possible
per run with scenario nodes; **Partial** — some of it; **No** — not possible without a code change to the
simulator. "Unknown" means the code was not conclusive; the row says what to check.

**Reading across the rows.** Answers and outgoing payloads are close to fully controllable (the precedence
chain, `beforeSend`, `ctx.raw`). Timings that the default behaviors own are params. What a model _advertises
about itself_ — its configuration, its limits, its topology — is where the simulator is weakest, and that is
exactly what a CSMS reads first.

### Where vehicle data comes from

- **Open EV Directory** ([entity page](../entities/open-ev-directory.md)), imported through the daemon or the
  console's [Import dialog](../entities/web-console.md#settings-page): per variant, the sources' specs side by
  side (`usableBatteryKwh`, `acMaxKw`, `acPhases`, `dcMaxKw`, AC/DC connector types) and DC curves with a
  quality grade. Nothing is merged or picked for you; the import is explicit and the values are **copied** into
  EV settings, so runs never depend on the catalog.
- **Twelve built-in presets** (`EV_PRESETS` in [`EVSettings.ts`](../../packages/core/src/cp/domain/connector/EVSettings.ts)):
  name, battery and maximum power only, flat curve.
- **Coverage.** What the catalog does not carry, the simulator cannot import: AC curves, voltages, currents,
  thermal data, ISO 15118 capabilities, V2G capability. How many variants have a usable capacity and a DC curve,
  and of which quality: unknown — check the catalog at <https://evdb.juherr.dev> (`/v1/variants/{id}/charging-curves`).
  Licences travel with the data (`attribution`, CC BY-SA 4.0 share-alike for EVDB records).

## Packaging a model and a vehicle today

**Charger model → blueprint.** A [blueprint](../concepts/control-plane.md#blueprints) is the closest thing to a
model profile: the `cp.create` block minus `cpId` — `vendor`, `model`, `ocppVersion`, `connectors`,
`bootNotification`, `transport`, `capabilityProfile`, `chargerCapabilities` (caps and meter realism),
`inboundValidation`, `behaviors` and `behaviorParams` — plus default `evSettings` and one
`scenarioTemplateId`. What it cannot carry:

- configuration keys and 2.x variable values, read-only or not, and vendor keys;
- a user scenario (the `scenarioTemplateId` must name a **built-in** template, checked with `getTemplateById` in
  [`socketServer.ts`](../../apps/cli/src/server/socketServer.ts)), so a `configSet` warm-up cannot ride along;
- `certQuirks` and `inboundPolicy` (scenario-scoped);
- the behavior code itself (only ids of behaviors already loaded with `--behavior`).

Blueprints are created with `blueprint.save` over the control plane only; there is no `--blueprint <file>` flag
(fleet templates have `--fleet-template`, and reference blueprints by id). The five built-ins (`ac-22kw` …
`dc-350kw`) are generic power classes, not makes.

**Vehicle → vehicle profile.** A [vehicle profile](../concepts/vehicle-profiles.md) is a reusable vehicle, but by
design a profile is copied, never referenced: a blueprint, a fleet member, a scenario or a `vehiclePool` entry holds
the resolved EV settings, not a profile id. That keeps runs offline and reproducible, at the cost of re-picking
after a profile edit.

**How close is "pick model X + vehicle Y and run"?** For a CSMS power or load test: close — save a blueprint
with the model's identity, caps and meter realism, paste the resolved vehicle settings into its `evSettings`
(or a fleet member's), `fleet.create`. For a CSMS integration test against a real model's personality: far —
the model's configuration has to be replayed with `configSet` nodes in every scenario (writable keys only), its
quirks written as behaviors and shipped next to the daemon, and its read-only keys stay the simulator's. There is
no catalog of real models, and no single file that one person can hand to another as "model X".

## Gap backlog

Ordered by impact: how many real-world test needs a CSMS team has that the item unlocks. "Small / medium /
large" is a rough size. Every item keeps rule 4 of the [engine](../concepts/charger-engine.md#rules) (no
configuration, no change on the wire) and the scenario budget of
[What the format will not do](../concepts/scenario-format.md#what-the-format-will-not-do): per-model and
per-vehicle knobs are **data on the station or the vehicle, or params of a behavior** — not new scenario nodes.

### Consistency with the scenario-language work

The in-flight scenario work — timeout edges (schema 1.7, branch `feat/scenario-timeout-edges`), parallel
branches and join, bounded / conditional / timed loops, and the block-tree editor (#860, #865, #869) — is what a
model package's `scenarios/` and per-EV test flows are written in; nothing above needs a new control-flow
construct. Two places touch the format, and both pass [the test for a new node](../concepts/scenario-format.md#what-the-format-will-not-do):

- **Changing the vehicle mid-run** (a second car on the same connector, a vehicle behavior switch) needs a
  `ctx` verb first (`ctx.setEvSettings` or a vehicle verb; `ctx` has none today), then a node over it. Until then
  a scenario's top-level `evSettings` sets the vehicle for the whole run.
- **Raising a model's fault** (item 11) is a verb on a built-in behavior, selected with `useBehavior` params.

Everything else — dwell times, firmware timings, vendor keys, vehicle policies — is data or behavior params, so
the 1.6 budget holds. Timed loops make "fault every N minutes, recover after M" and "pause / resume cycles"
expressible once they land; that is a reason not to add vehicle-specific timing nodes.

### Order

Items 1 → 2 → 3 are one track (the model package) and should go in that order: seeding is useful alone, vendor
declarations reuse its initialization path, the package format wraps both. Item 4 is independent and small per
key. Item 5 is a merge plus two small PRs. Items 6–10 are independent of each other; 6 (2.x multi-connector EVSE)
is the largest and should not block the rest.
